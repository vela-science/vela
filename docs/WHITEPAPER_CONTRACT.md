# Vela whitepaper evidence contract

Status: **working protocol-paper contract; not a breakthrough claim**

Working title:

> **Vela: Correction-Aware Scientific State Across Plural Authorities**

The paper will describe a real system and a falsifiable result. It will not
present Vela as equivalent to Git, Linux, Bitcoin, TCP/IP, or another
foundational system. Those projects set the bar for a small legible core,
explicit assumptions, independent implementation, operational evidence, and
honest limits.

## Claim in one sentence

The candidate claim is:

> Independently governed scientific repositories can exchange exact proposed
> state transitions, verify their causal inputs, apply local authority, replay
> the same bounded result, and localize the consequences of later corrections
> without a central writer or global consensus.

This claim is unproven. The
[protocol breakthrough benchmark](BREAKTHROUGH_BENCHMARK.md) defines the test.

## Nonclaims

The paper will not claim that:

- Vela determines scientific truth, novelty, importance, or ethics;
- one verifier pass implies acceptance;
- one Frontier's Decision governs another Frontier;
- all scientific relations fit one ontology or graph;
- Canopus is required to produce or replay Vela state;
- the Observatory, Neon, a Registry, or any hosted service is canonical;
- mathematics establishes transfer to every scientific domain;
- first-party repetitions establish independent adoption; or
- a benchmark win predicts the social impact of a foundational system.

## Research questions

### RQ1: Transition fidelity

Can two implementations validate the same proposed transition from exact prior
state and derive byte-identical bounded output?

### RQ2: Correction fidelity

Can both implementations identify every affected Claim, preserve every
unaffected or independently supported Claim, and open the same bounded repair
work after a real correction?

### RQ3: Authority containment

Can a second Frontier retain and check a foreign transition without changing
local Standing until its own authorized Decision?

### RQ4: Inheritance value

Can a cold successor locate the correction, decisive evidence, surviving
knowledge, and next correct action at least 20 percent faster than with Git
plus the same files and verifier?

### RQ5: Cost and removability

What storage, validation, authoring, review, and operational cost does Vela
add? Does replay still work after Canopus, the Observatory, Neon, caches, and
the original producer session are removed?

### RQ6: Product legibility

Can a cold user reconstruct the exact current Frontier map, distinguish
accepted, checked, recorded, disputed, rejected, and open state, and select one
valid next action without private maintainer context? Does the map update
correctly after a Decision without becoming canonical authority?

## Evidence ladder

| Level | Claim allowed | Evidence required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Vela records and replays local admission | Current canonical fixtures and completed Erdős loop |
| 1 | Two implementations agree on bounded correction impact | Primary real fixture and adversarial vectors |
| 2 | Vela demonstrates the registered protocol result | Primary and held-out fixtures, second Frontier, external cold use, all hard gates |
| 3 | The result recurs outside the project | Multiple organizations and independent implementations |
| 4 | Scientific state compounds routinely | Longitudinal correction, reuse, and adoption evidence |

The current evidence supports Level 0. Each result table must name the highest
level it supports.

### Current publication ceiling — 2026-08-02

The retained evidence is sufficient for a bounded engineering report, not the
registered protocol-breakthrough paper:

- attributed human Decisions, exact replay, and root-bound remapping pass on
  real Erdős and Quantum results;
- one first-party cross-Frontier package passes exact retention, two
  first-party scoped receiver Verifications, clean-clone replay, and zero
  imported Standing;
- three matched native Harbor tasks show task-specific first-party exactness
  advantages, while one Astra task failed and earns no lift claim; and
- Rust and dependency-free Python agree on the bounded synthetic
  correction-impact vectors.

The stronger paper remains gated because the post-Decision Quantum correction
has no preregistered hard dependent, support diamond, or non-consequential
relation; the held-out selector therefore retains
`no_qualifying_candidate` at byte root
`sha256:da87f6ebd438a3ef3e46c388ee7ff379a0bab74bc4db1713cf4a46de38952b52`.
No external participant has completed the cold-use fixtures, and no
organizationally independent implementation has reproduced the registered
result. Those absences must remain visible rather than being replaced by
first-party agents, synthetic topology, or a broader claim.

### Next evidence pass — 2026-08-03

The [active mathematical breakthrough campaign](CAMPAIGN.md) does not raise
this publication ceiling by declaration. It is designed to earn or falsify the
missing evidence through three linked cases:

1. exact inheritance of the external OpenAI Astra ten-advance release without
   equating native checking, statement fidelity, novelty, field review, or
   local Standing;
2. a complete Erdős 264 correction, proof repair, replay, remap, and
   cold-successor handoff; and
3. one preregistered research-level discovery attempt, initially Erdős 203
   under its merged Formal Conjectures statement, corrected exploratory
   lineage, and frozen exact verifier. Erdős 647 and Erdős 7 remain named
   reserves, not post-output substitutions.

One successful scientific case may justify a bounded case study. The stronger
product claim still requires unique scientific episodes, at least two task
families, a producer or model-family swap, complete registered denominators,
zero authority errors, and family-consistent continuation evidence. Repeated
calls on one source lineage cannot substitute for those conditions.

## Registered evaluation

The paper artifact contains one immutable evaluation plan with:

- hypotheses and exact expected outputs;
- primary fixture and held-out selection rule;
- baselines and assignment procedure;
- implementation and environment identities;
- correctness and safety gates;
- metrics and denominators;
- resource limits and stopping rules;
- permitted infrastructure retry;
- intervention and exclusion policy; and
- positive, negative, and partial publication rules.

### Benchmark families

| ID | Question | Release gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B1 | Are transition bytes and roots preserved across implementations? | Exact agreement |
| B2 | Are correction impact and repair sets complete and precise? | 100% precision and recall within the frozen bound |
| B3 | Do alternative support routes survive? | No over-invalidation |
| B4 | Can any lower-authority actor change Standing? | Zero unauthorized change |
| B5 | Are optional producers and readers removable? | Replay unchanged after deletion |
| B6 | Does hosted read-model failure affect verification? | No |
| B7 | Does route diversity reduce correction blast radius as declared? | Exact route accounting |
| B8 | Does cross-Frontier transfer retain identity without importing authority? | Exact import and zero local Standing delta |
| B9 | Are omissions, inaccessible inputs, and truncation explicit? | No silent completeness claim |
| B10 | Does Vela improve correct cold continuation over Git? | At least 20% median lift on both fixtures |

Every family includes positive, malformed, stale, substituted, truncated, and
authority-adversarial cases where applicable.

## Baselines

The primary baseline is:

```text
Git
+ the same source and evidence bytes
+ the same frozen verifier
+ ordinary repository documentation
```

The Vela arm receives no additional scientific facts. Both arms use the same
machine, source commit, participant instructions, time limit, and success
rubric. Record correctness before speed.

Execution comparisons report each native agent or workbench configuration
separately through a removable evaluation harness such as Harbor. They do not
substitute for the Git state baseline.

## Paper structure

1. **Abstract.** One problem, one mechanism, one measured result, one limit.
2. **Introduction.** Why abundant production creates an inheritance and
   correction bottleneck.
3. **Scope and model.** Frontiers, plural authority, exact objects, Standing,
   and trust assumptions.
4. **Protocol.** Submission, scoped Verification, Decision, Event, replay, and
   correction without erasure.
5. **Correction model.** Typed consequential relations, surviving routes,
   bounded impact, and repair Obligations.
6. **Implementation.** Rust reference, clean-room reader, Git custody, and
   removable producers and projections.
7. **Evaluation.** B1 through B10, map correctness and continuation, primary
   and held-out fixtures, baselines, raw results, and statistical treatment.
8. **Security and failure modes.** Producer, verifier, authority, publisher,
   reader, registry, truncation, and implementation threats.
9. **Related systems.** Exact borrowed mechanisms and the boundary Vela adds.
10. **Limitations.** Scientific judgment, ontology, external validity,
    governance, key compromise, and adoption.
11. **Conclusion.** Only the strongest sentence supported by the registered
    evidence.

## Artifact package

The paper is accompanied by a content-addressed source-only package containing:

- the exact paper source and rendered PDF;
- the evaluation plan and amendments;
- primary and held-out fixture bytes;
- source repository and release manifests;
- conformance and adversarial vectors;
- Rust and clean-room implementation source;
- build and dependency locks;
- raw command output, timing, correctness, and intervention logs;
- scripts or package commands for every table and figure;
- threat-model and authority audit;
- license and third-party notices;
- a short getting-started path that completes within 30 minutes;
- detailed full-reproduction instructions; and
- an artifact manifest with SHA-256 roots for every member.

The target is the standard expressed by modern systems artifact evaluation:
available, functional, documented, reusable, and independently reproduced.
The package must not require a private service, mutable URL, Vela credential,
human authority key, or original model session.

## Writing rules

- State requirements with normative language only where interoperability or
  safety needs them.
- Define every term before using it in a theorem, algorithm, or benchmark.
- Separate protocol rules, reference implementation behavior, observed
  results, and product interpretation.
- Give pseudocode for transition validation and bounded correction impact.
- Report asymptotic cost and measured cost.
- Include failure cases and negative results in the main paper where they
  change the claim.
- Use exact denominators. Do not use “orders of magnitude,” “100x,”
  “decentralized,” “verified,” or “independent” without the qualified
  measurement or actor.
- Do not cite the project's own architecture as evidence that the architecture
  works.

## Release gate

The protocol-paper version may be released only when:

1. ADR 0026 is Accepted;
2. the primary and held-out plans were frozen before confirmatory runs;
3. two implementations agree;
4. all hard safety gates pass;
5. both fixtures pass B1 through B10;
6. at least one external participant completes each cold-use fixture;
7. every result is reproducible from the public artifact package;
8. a mathematical domain reviewer checks the fixture interpretation;
9. a systems reviewer checks the protocol, threat model, and evaluation; and
10. the title, abstract, and conclusion contain no claim beyond the evidence
    ladder.

If these conditions do not pass, publish a narrower engineering report or a
falsification report. Keep the working paper out of the flagship editorial
surface.

## Systems-writing references

- [Git objects](https://git-scm.com/book/en/v2/Git-Internals-Git-Objects):
  a small content-addressed core with separate user-facing porcelain.
- [End-to-End Arguments in System Design](https://web.mit.edu/saltzer/www/publications/endtoend/endtoend.pdf):
  keep functions at the endpoints when only they have the knowledge required
  for complete correctness.
- [Build Systems à la Carte](https://simon.peytonjones.org/assets/pdfs/build-systems-original.pdf):
  isolate the design choices, define correctness, and compare systems in one
  executable framework.
- [RFC 2119](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/rfc2119/): reserve normative
  imperatives for interoperability and harm prevention.
- [Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System](https://bitcoin.org/en/bitcoin-paper.html):
  state the coordination problem, assumptions, mechanism, and quantitative
  analysis compactly. Vela does not adopt Bitcoin's consensus model.
- [NSDI artifact evaluation](https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi26/call-for-artifacts):
  align the artifact with paper claims and make it documented, complete,
  exercisable, and independently reproducible.
